# Opportunity Map

Updated: 2026-08-22
Status: COMPARISON COMPLETE / CLUSTER 01 SELECTED FOR PROBE DESIGN / NO CHANNEL AUTHORIZATION

The clusters below are research directions, not production decisions. Ranking is provisional until creator cases and observable channel histories are compared.

## Comparison dimensions

- strength of external demand signal;
- independent creator evidence;
- weak-competition or weak-packaging evidence;
- repeatability of the format;
- speed and cost of a falsification probe;
- fit with Remotion, AI assistance and automation assets;
- fit with owned external monetization;
- trust, policy and reputational risk;
- dependence on personality, celebrity access or paid traffic;
- durability and repeat-purchase potential.

## CLUSTER-SOL-01 — Russian personalized symbolic guidance

Audience need: people seeking structured self-reflection, compatibility or personalized symbolic interpretations in Russian.

Potential asymmetry: a useful personalized result delivered through an owned Telegram, site or bot flow may be more valuable than generic entertainment views.

Evidence state: one vendor-hosted Russian case describes an SEO-site calculator feeding a personalized Telegram bot, paid PDF and daily repeat-use loop. The author reports that Shorts, Reels and other video sources were comparatively ineffective, so the case supports the external-funnel architecture but does not support YouTube acquisition or conversion. The owner Telegram bots are operational production products and reduce implementation risk, but they do not establish YouTube demand, distribution or conversion.

Primary risks: guaranteed predictions, fear-based selling, medical or financial promises, weak trust and unverifiable conversion claims.

Current priority: `SELECTED FOR BOUNDED FALSIFICATION-PROBE DESIGN`.

## CLUSTER-SOL-02 — Hyper-specific high-intent practical guidance

Audience need: a concrete decision, task or recurring problem for which existing video answers are incomplete, outdated or poorly packaged.

Potential asymmetry: search or recommendation demand can lead to a calculator, bot, guide, template, subscription or other owned utility.

Evidence state: one secondary travel-business lead; independent cases required across unrelated verticals.

Primary risks: narrow topics without enough adjacent demand, search dependence and one-time visitors with no repeat value.

Current priority: `DEEP RESEARCH CANDIDATE`.

## CLUSTER-SOL-03 — Repeatable recognizable series

Audience need: a stable promise with variable episodes and clear expected payoff.

Potential asymmetry: reusable editorial and production structure can lower cost while building format recognition.

Evidence state: credible creator claims and observable examples exist, but causality and small-channel transfer remain unverified.

Primary risks: formula fatigue, interchangeable AI output and confusing production consistency with market demand.

Current priority: `EVIDENCE EXPANSION REQUIRED`.

## CLUSTER-SOL-04 — Underserved-question and weak-packaging arbitrage

Audience need: demonstrated interest served by old, vague, slow or visually weak competitors.

Potential asymmetry: research titles, thumbnails and promise structures before expensive production, then test only the strongest package-demand combinations.

Evidence state: discovery tools and creator anecdotes provide leads; decision-grade creator cases still need collection.

Primary risks: keyword-tool estimates without real viewing demand, transient trends and copying competitors without a distinct promise.

Current priority: `DEEP RESEARCH CANDIDATE`.

## CLUSTER-SOL-05 — Cross-platform demand transfer into an owned funnel

Audience need: a topic already producing repeated engagement on Telegram, TikTok, Reels, forums or search surfaces.

Potential asymmetry: use other platforms as demand sensors, not as proof that audiences will transfer; connect successful YouTube demand to an owned utility or relationship.

Evidence state: broad creator claims are common, but clean organic-transfer evidence is scarce.

Primary risks: paid seeding, non-transferable audiences, platform-specific formats and attribution loss.

Current priority: `LOW CONFIDENCE / SOURCE SEARCH`.
