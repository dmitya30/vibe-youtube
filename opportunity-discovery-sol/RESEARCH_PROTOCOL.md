# Research Protocol

Updated: 2026-08-22
Status: ACTIVE METHOD / INITIAL CASES PENDING VERIFICATION

## 1. Objective

Find permitted YouTube growth and conversion asymmetries already used by successful creators, verify them adversarially and convert only the strongest findings into cheap falsification probes.

Official YouTube materials are used primarily for policy, constraints and terminology. Generic platform advice is not accepted as evidence that a growth mechanism works.

## 2. Preferred source order

1. Creator-owned retrospectives with analytics or reproducible channel references.
2. Direct interviews, podcasts and public transcripts.
3. Public forum, Reddit, Telegram, X, Discord and community disclosures.
4. Observable channel history, upload patterns, public metrics and external links.
5. Independent case studies with disclosed methodology.
6. Secondary summaries used only as leads to primary evidence.

Search results, promotional landing pages and unattributed screenshots are not sufficient evidence by themselves.

## 3. Required classification

Every material statement must be labeled as one of:

- `OBSERVED FACT` — directly visible in a source, channel history or public metric.
- `CREATOR CLAIM` — asserted by the creator or operator but not independently established.
- `INFERENCE` — reasoned interpretation of facts and claims.
- `UNVERIFIED HYPOTHESIS` — plausible explanation awaiting evidence.
- `MARKETING CLAIM` — claim made while selling a course, service, tool or sponsorship-related offer.

A statement may have more than one label when, for example, a creator claim is also a marketing claim.

## 4. Required case fields

- case identifier;
- tactic or mechanism;
- primary URL;
- source author and relationship to the channel;
- publication date when available;
- capture date;
- claimed result and measurement period;
- observable public corroboration;
- channel-baseline comparison;
- recurrence within the same channel;
- recurrence across independent creators;
- possible paid-traffic or external-event effect;
- survivorship and selection bias;
- commercial conflict of interest;
- alternative explanations;
- applicability to available resources;
- external-monetization relevance;
- confidence level;
- evidence still needed;
- cheapest ethical falsification method.

## 5. Confidence levels

### HIGH

Primary evidence, meaningful observable corroboration and recurrence across at least two independent creators. Important alternative explanations have been tested or bounded.

### MEDIUM

A credible primary disclosure has partial observable corroboration, but recurrence or causal attribution remains incomplete.

### LOW

The claim is plausible but relies on self-reporting, a single outlier, weak public metrics or an unresolved commercial conflict.

### LEAD ONLY

The item is useful for further searching but cannot support an opportunity decision.

### REJECTED

The case depends on prohibited conduct, unverifiable income screenshots, misleading claims, purchased engagement, stolen or reused content, spam, fake identity, KYC evasion or metric manipulation.

## 6. Verification sequence

1. Capture the closest available primary source.
2. Separate the exact claim from interpretation.
3. Inspect public channel history and relative performance, not only lifetime totals.
4. Check whether the mechanism appears in several uploads.
5. Search for an independent creator using the same mechanism.
6. Test paid traffic, celebrity, news-cycle, collaboration and existing-audience explanations.
7. Identify course, agency, software, sponsorship and affiliate conflicts.
8. Record what evidence would falsify the proposed mechanism.
9. Score resource fit only after the evidence score is recorded.

## 7. Metrics

Distribution indicators may include subscribers, impressions, CTR, views, traffic sources, watch time, viewing depth, returning viewers and stable organic recurrence.

External-business indicators may include outbound clicks, leads, Telegram joins, bot starts, completed personalized results, conversion rate, revenue per 1,000 YouTube views, repeat purchases and contribution margin.

Large market estimates, revenue totals and screenshots require methodology and independent corroboration. YPP eligibility or AdSense revenue alone does not establish business fit.

## 8. Probe boundary

A probe must test one important uncertainty with minimal production cost. A long series of expensive videos is not an acceptable first probe. Probe design begins only after evidence comparison and does not authorize channel creation or publication.
