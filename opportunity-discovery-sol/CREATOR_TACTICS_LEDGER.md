## CASE-SOL-001 — Narrow editorial premise and aggressive removal of repetition

- Classification: `CREATOR CLAIM`, partial `OBSERVED FACT`, `INFERENCE`.
- Primary URL: https://creatorscience.com/is-shorter-better/
- Author: Jay Clouse / Creator Science.
- Publication date: 2023-07-16.
- Captured: 2026-08-22.
- Claimed result: two recent, approximately 34-minute interviews generated about 40 percent of first-year channel views and subscribers during the final 30 days.
- Public corroboration currently available: the article names the two videos and reports first-year upload, subscriber, view and watch-hour totals.
- Proposed mechanism: replace broad guest biography with one narrow viewer-relevant idea and remove repeated explanations.
- Alternative explanations: guest demand, title and thumbnail packaging, recommendation placement, audience growth and timing.
- Paid-traffic effect: not established.
- Recurrence: not yet established beyond two videos from one channel.
- Conflict: Creator Science sells creator education and memberships.
- Applicability: potentially high for package-first research and lower-cost editing.
- Confidence: `LOW` pending channel-baseline and independent recurrence checks.

## CASE-SOL-002 — Repeatable recognizable format as a path to consistency

- Classification: `CREATOR CLAIM`, `MARKETING CLAIM`, `INFERENCE`.
- Primary URL: https://podcast.creatorscience.com/colinandsamir/
- Authors: Colin Rosenblum and Samir Chaudry, interviewed by Jay Clouse.
- Publication date: to be captured during full verification.
- Captured: 2026-08-22.
- Claimed result: finding a format supports consistency and long-term brand recognition; revenue should not be treated as identical to channel performance.
- Public corroboration currently available: a full public transcript and an established channel with inspectable recurring formats.
- Proposed mechanism: a stable audience promise and episode construction reduce creative variance while building recognition.
- Alternative explanations: guest access, established brand, collaborations and team resources.
- Paid-traffic effect: not established.
- Recurrence: format recurrence is observable; causal effect is not yet established.
- Conflict: the episode promotes creator education products.
- Applicability: high for reusable production components, provided channel-specific identity remains distinct.
- Confidence: `LOW` pending format-level performance comparison and independent cases.

## CASE-SOL-003 — A single viral outlier is not a repeatable system

- Classification: `CREATOR CLAIM`, partial `OBSERVED FACT`, `INFERENCE`.
- Primary URL: https://www.reddit.com/r/juststart/comments/1aqkz0n/youtube_channels_case_study_update_months_1011/
- Author: Reddit user `PhilReddit7`.
- Publication date: 2024-02-14.
- Captured: 2026-08-22.
- Claimed result: two channels produced about 5.68 million lifetime views and GBP 14.3 thousand, but approximately 99 percent of the first channel traffic and revenue came from one video and follow-up attempts did not reproduce it.
- Public corroboration currently available: linked analytics screenshots and detailed monthly tables; ownership and full methodology remain unverified.
- Proposed lesson: outlier concentration must be measured before calling a tactic repeatable.
- Alternative explanations: topic-specific demand, recommendation shock, external coverage and undisclosed distribution.
- Paid-traffic effect: not established.
- Recurrence: explicitly weak in the reported first channel.
- Conflict: no commercial conflict identified in the captured post; further author review required.
- Applicability: high as a negative control for opportunity scoring.
- Confidence: `MEDIUM` for the methodological warning, `LOW` for exact financial claims.

## CASE-SOL-004 — Hyper-specific practical demand connected to digital products

- Classification: `CREATOR CLAIM` through a secondary source, `INFERENCE`.
- Primary lead URL: https://simonowens.substack.com/p/how-a-niche-youtube-channel-became
- Subject: Jessica Dante / Dante Media.
- Publication date: to be captured during full verification.
- Captured: 2026-08-22.
- Claimed result: practical London travel guidance expanded into a multi-platform business and high-margin digital products.
- Public corroboration currently available: only the public portion of a paywalled case study; channel history and product paths are inspectable leads.
- Proposed mechanism: answer high-intent, location-specific questions and connect viewers to owned practical products.
- Alternative explanations: tourism recovery, destination popularity, search seasonality and pre-existing professional expertise.
- Paid-traffic effect: unknown.
- Recurrence: not yet checked.
- Conflict: paid newsletter and case-study product.
- Applicability: conceptually high for useful personalized funnels, but vertical transfer is unproven.
- Confidence: `LEAD ONLY` until the primary interview and public channel evidence are captured.
