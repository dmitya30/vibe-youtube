Updated: 2026-08-07

## 1. Locked direction

- Angle: notifications off does not necessarily close the checking exit.
- Viewer problem: the phone is silent, but the viewer still checks it without
  an external prompt.
- Promise: distinguish incoming interruptions from self-initiated checking and
  provide one practical 25-minute experiment.
- Target duration: 8–10 minutes.
- Production target: approximately 5–7 days from topic lock.

Working packaging:

- title: `Turning Off Notifications Won't Fix Your Phone Habit`
- thumbnail concept: `STILL CHECKING?`

Title and thumbnail remain working assets until script and promise-match review.

## 2. Channel fit

VIDEO-001 established that a task can have external and self-created exits.

VIDEO-002 develops one self-created exit without repeating the general
task-switching explanation:

- VIDEO-001: reduce the number of exits around one task;
- VIDEO-002: silence removes an incoming prompt but may leave checking behavior
  available as an easy exit.

## 3. Claim table v0.1

| ID | Allowed claim | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| C01 | Receiving a phone notification can impair performance on a sustained-attention task even when the notification is not answered. | Do not generalize the measured effect to every task, person or notification. |
| C02 | In one preregistered randomized trial with 205 participants, disabling notifications for one week did not reduce objectively logged checking frequency or screen time. | Do not say disabling notifications never works. |
| C03 | In the same trial, participants reported lower checking-habit strength while objective checking frequency remained unchanged. | Subjective and behavioral measures must remain distinct. |
| C04 | Disabling notifications increased reported fear of missing out in that trial. | Do not claim this happens to every user. |
| C05 | Removing an incoming signal and changing self-initiated checking are different intervention targets. | Present this as the video's evidence-informed explanatory model. |
| C06 | In a randomized trial, blocking mobile internet on smartphones for two weeks improved measured sustained attention, subjective well-being and mental-health outcomes among participants who complied. | It was a stronger and longer intervention than the video's protocol; do not make medical promises. |
| C07 | Ninety-one percent of compliant participants improved on at least one of the three primary outcomes in that trial. | Do not convert this into “91% improved attention.” |
| C08 | A multi-strategy nudge intervention reduced problematic smartphone use and screen time and improved sleep quality relative to a control condition. | The study cannot isolate the effect of one component such as grayscale or notifications. |
| C09 | The Silent Door Test is an editorial self-experiment for one work session. | It is not a clinically validated treatment. |
| C10 | Approximately 86 daily checks was the baseline average in one specific study sample. | It is not a universal statistic and is not approved as the main packaging claim. |

## 5. Prohibited framing

Do not claim:

- notifications are harmless;
- silent mode is useless;
- everyone checks a phone 86 times per day;
- smartphones necessarily damage intelligence or the brain;
- all self-initiated checks are an addiction;
- dopamine explains the entire behavior;
- a 25-minute intervention is scientifically proven to improve attention;
- the research establishes universal causation outside its measured outcomes.

## 6. Accepted cold open v0.1

> You turned off every notification.
>
> No banners. No vibrations. No little red numbers asking to be opened.
>
> Your phone is silent, face down — exactly where you left it.
>
> And then, without hearing anything, you reach for it anyway.
>
> That matters, because notifications and checking are not the same problem.
> In a randomized one-week study, turning notifications off did not reduce how
> often people checked their phones — or their total screen time.
>
> Silence removed the interruption. It didn't close the exit.
>
> So what does?

## 8. Silent Door Test v0.1

For one 25-minute task block:

1. retain any genuinely necessary emergency channel;
2. disable non-essential notifications;
3. move the phone outside automatic reach;
4. temporarily disable mobile internet or selected distracting apps;
5. define the next allowed check time;
6. mark each urge to check without acting on it;
7. review the marks after the task block.

The protocol measures noticed checking impulses. It does not diagnose addiction
or promise a clinical outcome.
