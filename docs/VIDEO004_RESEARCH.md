## 1. Strategic correction

The first three public videos established production quality and a coherent digital-behavior cluster, but did not establish product-market fit.

VIDEO-004 must test a broader, already demonstrated viewer demand. Scientific usefulness alone is not a topic-selection criterion.

Operating principle:

> Demand selects the problem and earns the click. Evidence and execution fulfil the promise and earn viewer satisfaction.

This is competitor demand research, not copying. Public competitor retention is unavailable. Valid public proxies are:

- views relative to channel size and normal channel performance;
- view velocity when available;
- repeated success of the same problem across independent creators;
- recurrence of the format on the same channel;
- title and thumbnail archetypes;
- comments, likes and visible audience response;
- opening structure and runtime.

No claim about a competitor having high retention is permitted without non-public evidence supplied by that creator.

## 2. Demand clusters reviewed

### Procrastination and task initiation

Demand: high and evergreen.

Repeated packaging archetypes include:

- why you procrastinate even when it feels bad;
- you are not lazy;
- why you cannot start;
- how to act when you do not feel like it;
- stop wasting time or life.

This cluster combines broad applicability, strong emotional recognition and a practical behavioral deliverable.

### Social awkwardness and conversations

Demand: high.

Strong recurring desires include never running out of things to say, avoiding awkward silence and keeping a conversation going. This is retained as a leading VIDEO-005 or VIDEO-006 candidate.

### Feeling stuck, falling behind and wasting life

Demand: high, but generic motivational competition and guru-style overpromising create a substantial differentiation risk.

### Overthinking

Demand: high, but clinical ambiguity creates elevated claims risk around anxiety, rumination and intrusive thoughts.

### Phone, dopamine and attention

Demand: high, but VIDEO-001 through VIDEO-003 already test adjacent digital-behavior problems. A fourth consecutive phone-centered topic would provide weak information about the breadth of the channel.

### Learning and rereading

Demand: credible but narrower. Retrieval practice remains a strong future topic, but rereading is deferred because it is not the strongest mass-demand test for VIDEO-004.

## 3. Accepted VIDEO-004 direction

Primary cluster:

`PROCRASTINATION_AND_TASK_INITIATION`

Viewer moment:

> A meaningful task is open in front of the viewer. The viewer wants the result and understands that the work matters, but delays the first real action.

Leading working title:

`Why You Can’t Start — Even When You Want To`

Leading working thumbnail text:

`NOT NOW.`

The title and thumbnail are leading candidates, not locked publication metadata.

Alternative package candidates:

1. `Why You Avoid the Work That Matters Most` / `STILL WAITING?`
2. `How to Start When You Don’t Feel Like It` / `NOT MOTIVATED?`
3. `You’re Not Lazy — Your Next Step Is Too Vague` / `WHERE DO I START?`

## 4. Editorial promise

The video must not become a general lecture about procrastination.

The opening should show a recognizable behavioral moment:

- an important project is ready;
- the viewer genuinely wants to do it;
- a smaller substitute action suddenly feels urgent;
- the viewer waits for a clearer or more motivated state;
- the paradox is that desire for the outcome does not produce initiation.

The practical deliverable should identify the moment the task becomes aversive, uncertain or emotionally costly, then convert the task into a visible first action that can be attempted without waiting for motivation.

## 5. Claims boundary

Allowed:

- procrastination can involve short-term mood repair, task aversiveness, uncertainty, anticipated effort or fear of evaluation;
- different viewers may delay for different reasons;
- reducing ambiguity and the size of the first action may help initiation;
- the proposed method is a bounded experiment, not a universal treatment.

Prohibited:

- diagnosing ADHD, depression, anxiety disorders or executive dysfunction;
- claiming that all procrastination has one cause;
- promising permanent elimination of procrastination;
- claiming that motivation is irrelevant for everyone;
- using unsupported dopamine-reset or brain-hacking language.

## 6. Working channel core

Previous effective center:

`evidence-based explanations of attention and digital behavior`

Provisional expanded center:

> Visual explanations for the moments when you know what to do, but still do something else.

The expanded center includes distraction, checking, bedtime delay, task initiation, learning behavior, conversational avoidance and decision loops.

This channel-core wording remains provisional until evidence from approximately 6–8 public videos exists.
