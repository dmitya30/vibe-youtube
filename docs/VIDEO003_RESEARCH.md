## 2. Viewer problem

The viewer is already tired and may no longer be enjoying the feed, but continues scrolling instead of ending the day.

The video must distinguish at least two possible behaviors:

1. delaying the act of going to bed;
2. getting into bed but delaying sleep through phone use or another activity.

The script must not assume that every late-night phone user has insomnia, addiction, ADHD or a sleep disorder.

## 3. Working promise

Explain why late-night scrolling can continue after it stops feeling restorative, distinguish bedtime delay from while-in-bed delay, and provide one bounded experiment that creates a clearer stopping point before sleep.

The promise is behavioral and educational. It is not medical treatment.

## 7. Evidence table

| Claim | Evidence status | Allowed wording | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bedtime procrastination means going to bed later than intended without an external reason preventing it | Supported by the foundational study | Researchers use bedtime procrastination for delaying an intended bedtime when no external circumstance forces the delay | Do not apply the label to shift work, caregiving, illness or a sleep disorder |
| Bedtime procrastination is associated with self-regulation and insufficient sleep | Supported, primarily correlational | Studies link bedtime procrastination with lower self-regulation and self-reported insufficient sleep | Do not claim simple one-way causation |
| Delaying bedtime and delaying sleep while already in bed may be different behaviors | Supported by one exploratory adolescent study | Researchers have proposed separate bedtime and while-in-bed forms | Do not present this exploratory distinction as settled universal science |
| Electronic media use is associated with poorer sleep outcomes | Supported by systematic review and meta-analysis | Across many studies, heavier electronic-media use is associated with poorer sleep outcomes | Most included evidence is correlational; do not say every phone session damages sleep |
| A behavioral intervention reduced bedtime procrastination in a small young-adult trial | Supported by an open-label wait-list RCT | One small trial reported meaningful reductions after a behavioral intervention | Do not promise the same result to all viewers |
| Emotional regulation, compensation and social belonging can be functions of bedtime procrastination | Supported within the small intervention sample | In one study, participants reported several functions, including emotional regulation and compensation | Do not reduce everyone to one motive |
| Late scrolling is always revenge for a day without autonomy | Not established | Sometimes the delay may function like compensation for a day that did not feel personally owned | Must remain a possible interpretation, not the universal thesis |
| Infinite feeds remove natural stopping cues | Plausible editorial mechanism pending dedicated evidence | The feed always offers another item, so the viewer may need to create a stopping point | Do not label this a proven neurological mechanism without a source |
| Blue light is the main reason the viewer stays awake | Not approved | Screen light may be one factor among timing, displacement, arousal and content | Avoid blue-light reductionism |
| The viewer has phone addiction, insomnia or ADHD | Not approved without diagnosis | Describe the behavior rather than diagnose the viewer | No medical diagnosis or treatment claim |

## 8. Source record

### Foundational definition

Kroese et al., `Bedtime procrastination: introducing a new area of procrastination`

- community sample: N=177;
- cross-sectional survey;
- definition and association with self-regulation and insufficient sleep;
- authors explicitly caution against causal inference.

Source:

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4062817/

### Bedtime vs. while-in-bed distinction

Magalhães et al., `An Exploratory Study on Sleep Procrastination: Bedtime vs. While-in-Bed Procrastination`

- exploratory online study;
- 400 high-school students;
- low correlation between the two proposed facets;
- device activities in bed were common in the sample;
- results are not automatically generalizable to adults.

Source:

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7460337/

### Electronic media and sleep

`Electronic Media Use and Sleep Quality: Updated Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis`

- 55 papers and 41,716 participants;
- general media use and problematic media use were associated with poorer sleep outcomes;
- substantial heterogeneity;
- primarily correlational evidence;
- the authors state that causal inference is limited.

Source:

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11077410/

### Behavioral intervention

Jeoung et al., `A randomized controlled trial of a behavioral intervention for decreasing bedtime procrastination`

- 60 young adults;
- open-label wait-list design;
- treatment group showed reduced bedtime procrastination and improved sleep-efficiency measures;
- sample was small, young and predominantly female;
- the result is promising but not universal proof.

Source:

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37354745/

## 9. Editorial architecture

Provisional structure:

1. Cold open: the viewer is tired, no longer enjoying the feed and still scrolling.
2. Promise: explain why this can continue without blaming a broken brain.
3. Distinguish going-to-bed delay from while-in-bed delay.
4. Explain how an activity with no obvious ending can occupy the final part of the day.
5. Present compensation or emotional regulation as possible motives, not universal causes.
6. Show why a command such as `use more willpower` arrives at the weakest point of the day.
7. Offer one bounded stopping-point experiment.
8. Close with a concrete viewer question.

The architecture must not become a generic list of sleep-hygiene tips.

## 10. Cold-open gate

Target duration:

`10–15 seconds for conflict and promise`

Provisional cold-open direction:

`It is late. Your eyes hurt. You stopped enjoying the feed twenty minutes ago, and you are still scrolling. This is not always about being too awake. Sometimes, going to sleep feels like ending the only part of the day that was yours.`

This wording is not locked narration. It must pass editorial review for:

- speed;

- non-universal wording;
- title payoff;
- visual action;
- absence of diagnosis;
