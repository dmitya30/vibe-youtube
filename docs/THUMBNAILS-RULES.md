# NOD Thumbnail Rules

Updated: 2026-08-03
Status: draft / experimental system
Scope: channel-level invariants and VIDEO-001 packaging hypotheses

This document separates durable NOD identity rules from visual choices that
still require evidence from real publication tests.

## 1. Channel-level packaging invariants

Every thumbnail must have:

1. one viewer problem;
2. one dominant visual conflict;
3. one immediately identifiable subject;
4. an honest relationship to the title and opening;
5. readable silhouettes at mobile size;
6. minimal informational clutter;
7. a clear focal hierarchy;
8. no promise that the video fails to fulfil.

CTR alone does not determine success. Packaging is evaluated together with
early retention, watch time and viewer satisfaction.

## 2. NOD identity invariants

Use `nod-canonical-v1.jpg` as the only approved character-identity reference.

Preserve:

- the double-layer paper head;
- canonical silhouette and proportions;
- graphite contour language;
- tactile paper or cardboard material;
- simple dot eyes and line-based mouth;
- absence of realistic human skin.

The neutral canonical face is an identity reference, not the default
thumbnail expression.

Thumbnail expressions must remain active and readable on mobile.

Default exclusions:

- realistic human anatomy;
- medical brain or skull imagery;
- extra characters;
- distorted limbs;
- generic emoji styling;
- childish cartoon rendering.

Exceptions require an explicit editorial reason.

## 3. Action and emotion

NOD should normally be caught in an action rather than standing and looking.

Preferred action language for VIDEO-001:

- resisting;
- gripping;
- pulling;
- leaning;
- reaching;
- protecting the active task;
- being pulled by an external force.

Preferred emotional direction:

- alarmed expression;
- visible struggle;
- dynamic lean;
- magnetic pull energy;
- dramatic lighting.

Expression must be readable at approximately 160x90 pixels.

## 4. VIDEO-001 visual system

These rules belong to the current video package and are not permanent
requirements for every future channel thumbnail.

Palette:

- cream or desaturated beige background;
- graphite or deep charcoal structure;
- electric orange as the only saturated accent.

Orange represents:

- distraction;
- interruption;
- temptation;
- unresolved conflict;
- attention being pulled away.

Materials and lighting:

- tactile paper or cardboard NOD;
- matte studio background;
- visible grounding shadows;
- strong orange side or back rim light;
- cinematic rather than flat illumination;
- foreground, conflict object and background separation.

Motion energy:

- thin organic orange threads;
- curved magnetic flow;
- sparks and particles;
- clear direction of force.

Avoid straight arrows, detailed interfaces and generic neon UI strips.

## 5. Dominant conflict object

VIDEO-001 thumbnails use one external antagonist comparable to or larger than
NOD:

- a phone portal;
- a phone containing multiple exits;
- a giant timer.

The antagonist must remain readable after substantial downscaling.

This is a current packaging hypothesis, not a permanent channel constraint.

## 6. Text policy

Generate hero art without text, letters, numbers, logos or watermarks.

Add final typography deterministically in Remotion to guarantee:

- correct spelling;
- consistent font weight;
- exact positioning;
- safe margins;
- easy iteration;
- reproducible exports.

Thumbnail text should not merely repeat the title.

Current overlays:

- A: no text;
- B: `NOT YOUR BRAIN`;
- C: `00:47`.

Text does not have to occupy the upper-left corner. Placement must follow the
strongest composition. In C, the timer face is the intended text container.

## 7. AI generation workflow

For each concept:

1. generate one initial result;
2. if the concept works, make no more than two targeted edits;
3. preserve unexpectedly strong expressions and poses;
4. if the core concept does not read, reconsider the prompt instead of
   endlessly polishing;
5. do not generate multiple concepts inside one image.

Primary model for the current cycle:

`GPT Image 2.0`

This choice is based on the current generation results and is not assumed to
be permanently superior.

## 8. Pre-publication QA

Evaluate every candidate in:

- full 16:9 resolution;
- actual 160x90 mobile size;
- enlarged mobile preview;
- grayscale;
- mild blur;
- one-second recognizability;
- title and opening promise match.

Questions:

1. What is the first object perceived?
2. What action is occurring?
3. What is the source of conflict?
4. Is NOD's emotion readable?
5. Does the text survive mobile size?
6. Does the image create curiosity without creating a false promise?
7. Is the candidate meaningfully different from the other test variants?

## 10. Evidence status

The following remain hypotheses until real channel data exists:

- thumbnail packaging determines any fixed percentage of video success;
- maximum brightness always increases qualified clicks;
- negative framing universally outperforms positive framing;
- faces are mandatory;
- one fixed text position is optimal;
- the current orange-energy system should be repeated across future videos.

Record actual YouTube Test & Compare results before promoting any of these
choices into permanent channel rules.
