## 3. Description

Your attention span may not be broken. The task in front of you may simply
have too many exits.

This video explains what the “47-second attention span” finding actually
measured, why turning off notifications does not remove every impulse to
switch, and what makes interrupted work difficult to resume.

You’ll also learn the One Door Protocol — a practical, evidence-informed
experiment for making one task easier to continue and easier to return to.

Try it once:

1. Name one observable outcome.
2. Close visible exits.
3. Park unrelated impulses.
4. Leave a concrete return cue.
5. Stop at a natural breakpoint.

Then test the setup for 25 minutes and notice which exit you still try to
take.

This is an educational video, not medical advice. The One Door Protocol is
an editorial synthesis of interruption research, not a clinically validated
treatment.

Research and further reading:

Gloria Mark / American Psychological Association:
https://www.apa.org/news/podcasts/speaking-of-psychology/attention-spans

Mark, Gudith & Klocke — The Cost of Interrupted Work:
https://www.ics.uci.edu/~gmark/chi08-mark.pdf

Trafton et al. — Preparing to Resume an Interrupted Task:
https://www.interruptions.net/literature/Trafton-IJHCS03.pdf

Altmann & Trafton — Resumption Lag and the Role of Cues:
https://www.interruptions.net/literature/Altmann-CogSci04.pdf
